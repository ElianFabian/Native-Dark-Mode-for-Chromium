# Website theme rendering

Native delegates rendering to Chromium. Soft dark and High contrast apply palette roles. Simulated Native uses computed paint and a Chromium-derived color transform as a starting point, then adds contextual readability and interaction repairs. It intentionally aims to improve on Native where possible, rather than reproduce its output or its defects exactly. None of these renderers guarantees a correct result on every page.

## Product goals and boundaries

Prefer readable text and controls, retained useful colors, preserved image content and icon geometry, stable interaction states, reversible overrides, and responsive pages. Native parity is useful evidence, not the final acceptance criterion. A native rendering defect is not something Simulated Native must copy. Conversely, a custom repair is not automatically an improvement: measure the actual backing, test perception and interaction where practical, and include an unchanged negative case.

Simple ratios, perceptual heading polarity, and thresholds are implementation choices. Font weight, glyph size, anti-aliasing, transparency, saturated backings, and image variation affect perceived readability. These heuristics do not certify WCAG/APCA compliance. Exact Blink paint context, its image classifier, and all cross-origin pixels are unavailable to the content script.

The original prototype scope and pinned source are historical records in [the handoff](CHROMIUM-DARK-MODE-HANDOFF.md). The current policy is broader. [The roadmap](ROADMAP.md) records the possible future retirement of Native and the unimplemented local regression gallery.

## Lifecycle and shared rules

[controller.js](../extension/controller.js) serializes theme changes for each tab. The winning rule is based on the top-level URL. Custom themes apply in eligible frames through [website-theme.js](../extension/website-theme.js). Native uses the debugger command and clears its override before detaching. Keep a renderer active during theme handoff so the page cannot briefly show its light canvas.

Sample authored styles with the paint stylesheet disabled for a full viewport refresh. For smaller updates, temporarily remove the paint markers on the elements being sampled so Chromium does not have to recalculate styles for the entire page. Keep paint-related transition guards active during both kinds of sampling. Prioritize visible content, coalesce pointer changes into one frame, and use idle batches for offscreen content. Ignore inline updates that only move an element, such as transforms written on every frame by carousel libraries. Remove style sheets, markers, observers, and scheduled work when a custom theme is cleared.

## Separators and borders

One level belongs to each saved website or path rule and is shared by the three custom themes. The six choices are Off, Very low, Low, Medium, High, and Maximum. Native retains the saved preference for later use with a custom theme.

Border emphasis is separate from surface separation. Neutral article/aside cards and raised card links can receive a subtly distinct dark surface even with emphasis Off. Soft neutral outer card shadows can become low-opacity light halos while retaining their offsets/blur/spread; inset shadows and colored shadows retain their source policy. Stronger emphasis adds its own inset edge, preserving dimensions and focus indicators.

Off keeps the base palette in Soft dark and High contrast. Simulated Native can soften bright decorative separators in Off to resemble Chromium. At higher levels, prioritize thin structural lines and the edges of cards that blend into adjacent surfaces. Preserve focus rings, selected states, and meaningful control borders. Maximum can draw a wider inset edge on cards. Avoid adding layout-affecting border widths when an inset effect can provide the same separation.

Adapt visible border sides individually. A transparent border can reserve space for a selected or hovered state, and recoloring it makes that space visible. Keep neutral tab borders that use `currentColor` separate from text contrast corrections. Add a card outline only when the element has no visible border, border image, or authored outline.

## Site-independent fixes

Local regression evidence is indexed in [the rendering lab](RENDERING-LAB.md) and its [specimen provenance](../fixtures/diagnostics/README.md). The extended collection reconstructs icon/image/control/border/surface/state patterns from reported pages with generic markup and synthetic assets. Coverage mappings distinguish partial reproductions from automated assertions; they do not imply that every live page/state was rechecked.

- Ignore shapes inside SVG definitions, clipping paths, and masks when classifying visible icon colors. Their fill controls geometry rather than the displayed glyph. Reinspect a compact SVG control and its descendants together when hover changes its backing, and queue visible repainting when an observed accessibility visibility or expansion state changes.
- Prefer semantic elements, accessibility state, geometry, and computed paint. Treat generic names such as `logo` and `avatar` as hints alongside paint and control structure.
- Preserve the source drawing of icons and images wherever possible. Inspect whether a bright layer comes from a background, a pseudoelement, a blend mode, or a transition before changing it.
- Classify pagination dots by their rendered marker and hidden numeric label. Read the selected state from accessibility attributes or a visual distinction already supplied by the page.
- Adjust a decorative title gradient when the computed background consists of CSS gradients, or when a CSS gradient mask shapes an empty decorative layer. Keep its geometry, alpha, and authored color accents.
- Measure text over separate image layers using the pixels behind each line. Preserve readable authored colors. Change the ink before adding a local backing. Use a backing when the sampled region varies too much or the browser cannot expose its pixels. Bound thumbnail work and cache it by image source.
- When reproducing a website issue locally, include the relevant computed paint and test a similar case that should retain its original styling. Record browser checks separately from screenshots or fragments supplied by the reporter.

Browser restrictions, including extension stores and built-in viewers, belong in access detection. They are separate from website paint decisions.

## What belongs in shared code

| Area | Placement |
| --- | --- |
| Document and frame lifecycle, Shadow DOM, viewport scheduling, hover resampling, and stylesheet cleanup | Share among the custom themes. A change to timing or teardown must work for both CSS renderers. |
| DOM classification and paint measurements | Share when both renderers need the same facts. Examples include sprite and mask detection, monochrome SVG inspection, decorative backgrounds, alpha compositing, and contrast ratios. |
| Backdrop inheritance | `textBackdrop` walks parents, assigned slots, and shadow hosts for both renderers. Simulated Native projects authored paint through its color transform. Palette repairs read the rendered palette. Local sampling keeps authored ancestor backgrounds and the display/shadow/border/radius context used by surface classification in a weak cache. Direct and cached parent projection must choose the same surface; otherwise a child glyph can alternate contrast against an invisible page canvas instead of its visible card. Adapted decorative SVGs remain recognized on later passes. |
| SVG glyphs, generated control glyphs, and placeholders | `svgIconPaint`, `controlGlyphPaint`, `labeledControlGlyphs`, `expanderPaint`, and `placeholderPaint` serve all three custom themes. Theme policy supplies the backdrop and text target. A nested SVG glyph can use an opaque circle or rectangle painted behind it as its own backdrop. |
| Separator preference | Share the rule setting and levels. Each renderer decides how to reach the requested emphasis while preserving its own default. |
| Palette roles and post-paint contrast repair | Keep together for Soft dark and High contrast. This code measures the rendered palette and can assign a separate surface or edge to a card. |
| Authored color conversion and special paint handling | Keep in Simulated Native when the operation depends on its predictions or Chromium's behavior. This includes color conversion, perceptual selection for large headings, preserving SVG paint relationships, and softening decorative separators in Off. |

Share the evidence and calculations before sharing the paint policy. For example, both renderers can recognize the same sprite and measure the same contrast ratio. Simulated Native may preserve the sprite's colors or invert the original pixels, while a palette theme may use a light or dark icon role. Keep the native debugger path separate from both CSS renderers.

## Resource reads and conservative fallbacks

The renderer has no telemetry endpoint. It may inspect resources already referenced by the page. `readImageResource` bounds a read to 512 KiB and two seconds; cross-origin CORS reads omit credentials and same-origin reads may include existing page credentials. The resource host can observe the request. A failed read, CORS denial, unsupported decoder, or ambiguous classification must not trigger forced recoloring.

Image-text overlay sampling bounds the thumbnail to 96px per side, 48 word ranges and 16,384 pixel visits per label. The separate artwork classifier bounds its thumbnail to 320px and 16,384 pixels. Static-artwork decisions inspect format/frame eligibility and cache results. A small transparent SVG image beside a short label may receive a reversible CSS content override for a simple two-to-four-ink static drawing, with each low-contrast ink adapted independently. This bounded path requires readable original pixels, a known originally light background, no active/external SVG content and retained source/path geometry; it does not filter the whole image or rewrite its src. Transparent multicolor wordmarks can recover a light backing; confidently static monochrome artwork can receive a tint, including a colored single-ink image-only link under the stricter silhouette checks described above. External SVG control backgrounds are limited to compact, empty, accessible controls, a 4 KiB simple monochrome SVG, and a bounded per-document cache. They reuse the existing glyph recoloring path and retain path geometry. Complex SVGs, multicolor drawings, photos, and arbitrary large images do not qualify for that path. Compact crop verification is limited to 48 by 48 displayed pixels, simple static SVG up to 32 KiB or verified single-frame PNG/GIF, at most one million source pixels, 128 cached results and eight pending requests per document. Inspect dimensions before decoding and cache unsupported results; cross-origin reads remain credential-free and CORS-bound.

## Audit and remaining limits

The production renderer has no hostname branches or selectors for Amazon, Indeed, BOOTH, or a particular component library. It still uses generic naming hints for logos, avatars, flags, breadcrumbs, and carousel direction. These hints can be incomplete or ambiguous. Combine them with structure and computed paint, and add a contrasting reproduction when broadening them.

Explicit Chrome Web Store hostnames and the built-in PDF viewer identifier occur in access detection. They describe browser restrictions and do not customize website paint.

The theme code remains in one content script so stylesheet ownership, markers, transition guards, and Shadow DOM cleanup share one lifecycle. Shared calculation helpers live alongside both renderers. A larger file split would need to preserve script injection order and initialization in existing tabs. The architecture does not require making the palette and Simulated Native paint policies identical.

### Perceived line weight in dark rendering

Preserve the visual hierarchy of authored boundaries, not just their CSS width. A 1px border can become nearly invisible when a blanket opacity reduction is applied to an already-dark source. Inspect source/applied color, alpha, composited contrast and geometry before deciding that a thicker stroke is necessary. Keep authored dark-surface edges intact; use modest measured color/contrast compensation for converted light-surface edges and explicit preference levels for stronger separation. Preserve transparent reservations, selected colors and focus feedback. Do not apply a universal stroke-width multiplier or add artificial outlines to text. Existing text policy changes foreground contrast while retaining source font weight, size, letter geometry and source shadows.

Material's [dark-theme design guidance](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/codelabs/design-material-darktheme) discusses tonal choices, text emphasis and surface hierarchy. It does not justify treating every text glyph or separator as needing a thicker outline. Our source-relative border compensation is an engineering policy and is not a validated universal optical-equivalence model.

The reported laboratory card was already dark in its authored CSS. Owned Brave/Windows measurement found unchanged 1px geometry but a Simulated Native alpha reduction to 0.2, reducing its composited edge/surface ratio from about 3.21 to 1.24. After the source-aware repair, Off retains 3.21, and Very low through Maximum increase it progressively (about 4.12, 5.16, 6.42, 7.81 and 10.28 in that local run) without changing its 1px width. The local border specimen includes a muted blue dark-source card and a deliberately strong dark-source frame as a preservation control. All six levels and all custom themes retain geometry; the Simulated Native-specific attenuation regression also asserts original Off paint and nondecreasing emphasis.
